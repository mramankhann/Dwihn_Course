## Why this matters

Dashboards let teams monitor several measures and examine differences across time or location. The proposal calls for a first program dashboard and a ZIP-code service-gap exercise. Lumenore's public materials describe filtering and visual exploration, but the specific DWIHN dashboard and map availability must be verified. A map is a prompt to investigate an inequity or access issue, not proof of its cause. ## Outcomes and preparation

Choose a suitable chart, configure or sketch a useful dashboard, interpret ZIP-level differences, and check small-cell privacy before sharing. Bring your S5 validation log and the [synthetic data](/resources/synthetic-data).

## Teach: build from a decision

Start with the decision a manager must make, such as “Where should we investigate follow-up barriers?” Then select a small set of views:

| View | Question answered | Review required |
|---|---|---|
| KPI card: current rate | What is the headline value? | Exact definition, period, numerator, denominator |
| Trend line | Is the change persistent or a one-week fluctuation? | Comparable periods, missing weeks, seasonality |
| ZIP table or map | Where is the difference concentrated? | Population size, geographic assignment, small cells |
| Breakdown by service type | Is the pattern consistent across programs? | Case mix and permitted disclosure |

Use consistent labels and units. A chart should tell the reader **what**, **who**, **when**, and **where the data came from**. A darker map area may simply have more eligible people. Compare **rates and counts together**, and avoid visual scales that exaggerate small differences.

### Service-gap reasoning

In the synthetic table, ZIP 48205 changes from `71/110 = 64.5%` to `65/110 = 59.1%`. The **5.4 percentage-point** decline is a signal. It does not prove that services were unavailable or that one team performed poorly. Test data completeness, referral pathways, appointment capacity, transportation, eligibility mix, and the measure's definition. Invite people who know the workflow to interpret the chart.

Before displaying a ZIP breakdown, check whether small groups might disclose information. The exercise's cell of two is a warning example; DWIHN's actual small-cell rule must govern exports and presentations.

## Instructor demonstration

In an approved training environment, open or build a dashboard with the synthetic rate, trend, and ZIP table. Show how a filter changes all views, then deliberately leave one chart on a different period. Ask learners to find the inconsistency. If map functionality is unavailable, use a ZIP table; the decision skill is the same. End by showing where the underlying counts or source details can be inspected.

## Guided practice
Create a dashboard sketch or sandbox view for the fictional program:

1. State the **decision question** at the top.
2. Include headline rate, trend, and ZIP breakdown.
3. Add the metric definition and a note naming the synthetic source.
4. Mark the lowest current rate and a plausible **investigation question**.
5. Identify what you would **hide, aggregate, or withhold** before a board export.

Exchange views with another group. Their task is to find one missing definition, one possibly misleading visual choice, and one privacy risk. Revise the view.

### Knowledge check

Why is “48205 has the lowest rate” an observation, while “48205 residents lack services” is an unproven explanation? What number should accompany a rate on the dashboard?

<details><summary>Check your reasoning</summary>

The rate can be calculated from the synthetic table. The cause requires further evidence and workflow knowledge. Show the numerator and denominator (or at least denominator) so the reader can judge the scale and stability of the rate.

</details>

## Takeaway and work product

Keep your dashboard view or annotated sketch and a two-sentence interpretation that separates **observation** from **hypothesis**.
