## Why this matters

The course now moves from isolated prompts to an end-to-end workflow. AI can accelerate a draft, report outline, or meeting recap, but the staff member must decide whether the task is permitted, compare output with source material, edit it, and route it for normal approval.

## Outcomes and preparation

## Teach: the six-step daily workflow

1. **Define the decision or communication.** Identify audience and owner. Is AI assistance actually useful?
2. **Classify and confirm.** Check the data tier, tool catalog, account, feature, and sharing path.
3. **Prepare minimal input.** Remove unrelated details; use synthetic data for practice.
4. **Prompt and draft.** Use Role, Task, Context, Format, Constraint.
5. **Review against sources.** Check names, dates, numbers, policy language, tone, accessibility, missing information, and unintended implications.
6. **Approve and record.** Follow the same human sign-off and retention rules as any other work product.

The last two steps are the difference between an AI-assisted workflow and an unreviewed AI output. A fast result is valuable only if it is safe and correct enough for its purpose.

## Role applications

| Role | Candidate task | Specific review |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinator | Draft a **synthetic** follow-up letter | Confirm appointment facts, member-centered language, translation quality, and clinical approval. |
| Program manager | Summarize a weekly performance table | Check measure definitions, denominator, period, exceptions, and the chart source. |
| Admin / operations | Draft an internal memo | Check policy version, required approver, audience, and non-public content. |
| Compliance | Turn a known policy excerpt into a checklist | Confirm each checklist item maps to the source; do not invent obligations. |
| Leader | Prepare a meeting brief | Distinguish observation, interpretation, proposed action, owner, and follow-up. |

The proposal mentions translation and Teams AI action items. Both are **configuration- and policy-dependent**. A machine translation of member communication needs a qualified review and an approved workflow before sending. Meeting recordings and summaries can contain sensitive content and require the current recording, access, and retention rules.

## Instructor demonstration

Use an invented appointment reminder with a wrong weekday in the AI draft. Show a redline: source says **Tuesday, June 9**, draft says **Thursday, June 9**. Mark the error, correct it, and note the source. Then remove an unsupported sentence promising a transportation benefit. Ask learners which error is easier to miss: the wrong weekday or the plausible benefit promise.

## Guided practice
Select one scenario below and produce a two-part artifact: a final edited output and a review log.

- **Coordinator:** draft a plain-language follow-up letter from synthetic appointment facts.
- **Manager:** write a 100-word weekly trend summary from a synthetic table, including the denominator.
- **Admin:** draft a three-bullet internal memo from a supplied synthetic policy excerpt.
- **Call center:** convert a synthetic interaction summary into a handoff note, preserving unresolved questions.
- **Compliance:** create a checklist from a synthetic policy excerpt and cite the line supporting each item.

The review log should have four columns: **AI claim**, **source checked**, **change made**, **human reviewer**. If you cannot verify a claim, remove or label it. Add one sentence explaining the tool and data-tier decision needed before doing a similar task with live data.

### Knowledge check

1. What should you do when an AI-generated report contains a number that is absent from the source?
2. Can an AI draft of a policy memo bypass the usual approval owner?

<details><summary>Check your reasoning</summary>

1. Remove or flag the number and find an authoritative source; do not repeat it as fact.
2. No. The normal accountable review and approval still apply.

</details>
