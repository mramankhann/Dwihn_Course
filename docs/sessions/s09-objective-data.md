## Why this matters

Two teams can bring different numbers to the same meeting and both be using honest calculations. The difference may be a denominator, date window, refresh time, or population filter. “Single source of truth” is a **governed definition and traceable dataset**, not simply one attractive dashboard. DWIHN's public quality-improvement materials show why consistent measures and coordinated review matter. [DWIHN Quality Improvement](https://dwihn.org/provider-resources/quality-improvement)

## Outcomes and preparation

Define a KPI fully, reconcile an apparent data conflict, and explain a trend without overstating cause. Review the [synthetic data](/resources/synthetic-data).

## Teach: a metric is more than a number

A usable KPI definition records **name, purpose, numerator, denominator, eligible population, exclusions, time window, update frequency, source system, owner, and known limitations**. For the synthetic follow-up rate, the numerator is completed follow-ups within seven days and the denominator is qualifying discharges. If one report uses all discharges and another excludes ineligible cases, their rates will differ. The meeting should resolve the definition before choosing an action.

| Report A | Report B | Reconciliation question |
|---|---|---|
| 73.0% current-quarter rate | 70.0% latest-week rate | Are the periods different? |
| 292 completions | 73.0% rate | Is one a count and the other a rate? |
| 59.1% for ZIP 48205 | 73.0% overall | Is one filtered to a subgroup? |
| “Updated Monday” | “Updated Thursday” | Did new records arrive or get corrected? |

### Read a trend carefully

The weekly synthetic counts rise from 42 to 84 between Weeks 3 and 4, while both weeks have a 70.0% rate. Higher volume does not necessarily mean improved performance. Compare like with like, check data completeness, and mark unusual events or definition changes. Avoid claims of cause from a line chart alone.

### Present with uncertainty

Use the sentence pattern: **“We observed X in source Y for period Z. This could reflect A or B. We will check C before deciding D.”** It separates observation from interpretation and action. An honest uncertainty statement makes a decision stronger by exposing what remains to be tested.

## Instructor demonstration

Show two synthetic slides: one reports “292 completed,” another “73% completed.” Ask whether they conflict. Then reveal the shared denominator of 400. Show a second conflict where the date ranges differ, and ask learners to create a metric definition card. If using Lumenore, inspect the data source and filters visibly rather than narrating them from memory.

## Guided practice
Your group receives the [weekly subset](/resources/synthetic-data). Write a KPI definition and a three-sentence briefing for a manager. The briefing must include one count, one rate, the period, one limitation, and one question for the data owner. Then reconcile a colleague's statement: “Performance doubled from Week 3 to Week 4 because completions doubled.” Explain what the weekly denominators show.

### Knowledge check

1. Why can a count and a rate move differently?
2. Which three fields would you check first when two dashboards disagree?

<details><summary>Check your reasoning</summary>

1. A count changes with the size of the eligible population; a rate divides by that population.
2. Start with measure definition/denominator, date range/data freshness, and active filters; the source and exclusions may also matter.

</details>
