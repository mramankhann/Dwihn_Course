## Why this matters

Staff can benefit from AI only when they understand the kind of help it provides. A fluent answer can save drafting time and still contain a wrong date, invented source, or inappropriate recommendation. DWIHN work involves access to behavioral health and crisis-related services, so the cost of an unchecked error can be high. The professional using the output remains responsible for the final communication or decision.

## Outcomes and preparation

By the end, explain in plain language how a generative model produces an answer, name three useful work tasks, identify two tasks that require human judgment, and demonstrate a basic output review. No prior knowledge is required. Before class, write one task you hope AI could help with and one concern you have.

## Teach: a useful mental model

AI is software that detects patterns in data and uses them to predict, classify, generate, or recommend. **Generative AI** creates text or other content from patterns learned in training and context supplied in a prompt. It predicts a plausible next part of an answer; it does not observe your workplace directly, know the current DWIHN policy automatically, or understand a person in the human sense. **Analytics AI** can help query or flag patterns in a defined dataset. Its answer depends on the connected data, definitions, permissions, and date range.

Think of AI as a fast drafting and analysis assistant. You supply the purpose and boundaries; it offers a candidate; you verify it. The better the source material and review, the more useful the candidate can be.

| Useful assistance | Why it helps | Required human check |
|---|---|---|
| Drafting a routine email from approved, non-sensitive facts | Reduces blank-page time | Verify facts, tone, audience, and authorization |
| Summarizing a long document in an approved environment | Surfaces main points quickly | Compare the summary with the source; check omissions |
| Exploring a governed dashboard | Makes trends easier to find | Check definitions, filters, data freshness, and subgroup size |
| Suggesting call-center knowledge articles | Helps locate relevant guidance | Confirm that the article is current and fits the caller's need |

AI should **not** make a diagnosis, determine crisis level, approve a benefit, make a rights decision, or send a member communication on its own. It also should not receive data in a tool or account that has not been approved for that information. These are examples of work requiring professional or organizational authority, not a complete policy list.

### Why confident errors happen

A model is optimized to produce a response, not to guarantee truth. It may fill a gap with a plausible claim, misread a number, omit a qualifier, or repeat bias in the source. In an analytic system, a wrong filter or measure can produce a technically correct chart for the wrong question. **Automation bias** is the human tendency to trust a polished machine output too readily. The control is to ask, “What source proves this?” and “What would change my conclusion?”

## Instructor demonstration

Show this **synthetic** request: “Draft a short reminder for a person whose follow-up appointment is Friday at 2 p.m.” Compare two candidate drafts. One invents a clinic address; the other leaves the address blank and asks the sender to verify it. Highlight the invented detail. Demonstrate a review by marking each fact as **source-confirmed**, **needs confirmation**, or **remove**. Then show a dashboard sentence, “Follow-up completion improved by 15%,” and ask whether “15%” means percentage points or relative change.

## Guided practice
In pairs, choose one of these invented situations:

1. An administrator drafts an internal meeting agenda from non-sensitive bullet points.
2. A coordinator receives a machine summary of a synthetic care note.
3. A manager sees a sudden drop in a weekly service metric.

For your case, write: **the useful AI task**, **two possible errors**, **the source you would check**, and **the human who owns the next action**. Do not use live records. Share a brief explanation with the class.

### Knowledge check

1. Does a well-written answer prove that the facts are correct?
2. What is the difference between a generated draft and an approved decision?
3. Name one reason a chart could answer the wrong question.
