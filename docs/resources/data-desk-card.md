# Data classification desk card

The four tier names come from the Netlink proposal. **DWIHN must approve definitions and allowed destinations before this card is used for real classification.** These are conservative training examples.

| Tier | Provisional example | Question before AI use |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | A currently published service description | Is this exact content already public, and is the destination approved? |
| **Internal** | A non-public agenda without member details | Is this workspace approved for internal business information? |
| **Confidential** | A draft contract, staff performance detail, or non-public plan | Who may access it, and is this feature specifically allowed? |
| **Restricted** | Identifiable member or behavioral health information | Is there a documented, role-authorized workflow for this data and task? |

## How to classify

1. Identify every kind of information present, including hidden metadata and attachments.
2. Use the **highest** tier present. Combining low-sensitivity fields can increase sensitivity.
3. Check the actual DWIHN policy definition and owner. Do not rely on the analogy alone.
4. Check the approved tool catalog for that tier and workflow.
5. If uncertain, stop and ask. Do not “test” a tool with the real data.

### Wallet analogy

A public flyer can be left on a community table. A staff agenda belongs inside the organization. A contract draft belongs with the authorized team. A member record is closer to a locked wallet: it contains details whose combination can be highly sensitive. The analogy helps memory; it is not a legal or DWIHN policy definition.

## Three quick cases

- **Published helpline description:** likely Public if copied from a verified public page. Confirm version and destination.
- **Internal staff roster:** at least Internal and possibly a higher tier under DWIHN policy. Check before use.
- **Member care note:** treat as Restricted in this training model. Use only a specifically authorized workflow and role.
