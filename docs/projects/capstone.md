# Capstone · From safe input to accountable action

**Use this synthetic case in S10–S12. No live member information is needed.**

## Scenario

You are on a cross-functional DWIHN program team. A manager asks whether a follow-up workflow needs attention. The team has the [synthetic follow-up dataset](/resources/synthetic-data). In the current quarter, the overall illustrative seven-day follow-up rate is `292/400 = 73.0%`; it was `298/400 = 74.5%` in the prior quarter. ZIP 48205 shows `65/110 = 59.1%` now, compared with `71/110 = 64.5%` before. A proposed service-type drill-down contains a cell of two people.

A draft AI narrative states:

> “DWIHN follow-up fell by 5.4%, because staff in 48205 failed to schedule appointments. The board should immediately reassign two coordinators. The small-cell chart is safe because names were removed.”

While preparing the report, an analyst realizes that a link to the small-cell chart may have been shared with a broad Teams group. This is an invented scenario; do not reproduce it with real information.

## Your task

Produce a **one-page decision brief** and an **incident first-response record**. You may work from the printed table; a live AI or analytics account is not required.

1. **Classify and choose.** State the likely highest tier for the chart under the provisional course model and identify the exact DWIHN catalog/policy facts needed before any live upload, AI summary, or export. If approval is unknown, write **stop and ask**.
2. **Correct the narrative.** Identify at least four unsupported or misleading claims. Replace them with a source-checked paragraph stating the right magnitude and uncertainty.
3. **Analyze fairly.** Give two plausible explanations for the ZIP change and the evidence needed to test each. State why the small cell should not be released until the actual DWIHN rule is applied.
4. **Choose an action.** Propose a proportionate first step, owner, review date, and success measure. Distinguish the observation from the decision.
5. **Respond to the link.** List the immediate action, reporting route, essential facts to preserve, and who determines notification or remediation. Avoid copying the chart into a new message.

## Submission template

```text
Decision question:
Source, definition, period, filters:
Observation with numerator and denominator:
What remains uncertain:
Proposed check/action, owner, due date:
Measure and date to revisit:
Data tier and approved tool/export decision:
AI narrative corrections:
Possible incident: timeline, containment request, report route, preserved facts:
```

## Scoring rubric

| Criterion | Points | Strong performance |
|---|---:|---|
| **Safety gate — critical** | 20 | Classifies data provisionally, asks for official DWIHN rule, confirms exact destination or stops. |
| **Evidence and arithmetic** | 20 | Gives `74.5% → 73.0%` overall and `64.5% → 59.1%` in 48205 as percentage-point changes; cites counts. |
| **Output review — critical** | 15 | Rejects unsupported cause, staff blame, action, and small-cell safety claim. |
| **Privacy of small cell — critical** | 10 | Does not release the cell; seeks DWIHN suppression/release decision. |
| **Decision quality** | 15 | Tests alternative explanations and names a proportionate action, owner, and follow-up measure. |
| **Incident response — critical** | 20 | Stops further sharing, reports promptly through the current channel, preserves facts securely, and leaves formal assessment to authorized staff. |
| **Total** | **100** | Proposed passing standard: 80 points **and every critical step**; DWIHN approves final rules. |

### Instructor discussion prompts

- What does the table actually prove, and what must still be investigated?
- If a chart is correct but uses the wrong date filter, is the board narrative reliable?
- What would you change if the broad link were never opened? Would you still report the near miss under current policy?
- How could a quality-improvement action be evaluated without blaming a neighborhood or individual staff member?
